Route for International Applicants — USA & Canada

For internationally educated nurses · Verified 17 November 2026

A nurse trained outside the USA or Canada can work in either country — but the routes are different, and the order of the steps matters. This page covers both countries: every route, every fee, and the exact first steps. Watch the full video guide, then read the details below.

Before you start: the three blockers

Most internationally educated nurses are held back by the same three things: a gap since their last clinical job, no English test yet, and no NCLEX yet. Everything on this page fixes one of those three — and the first step is the same for both countries: an English test, the NCLEX exam, and recent hospital practice.

USA — Route 1: EB-3 Green Card (Schedule A)

Permanent residence, sponsored by your employer. Nurses sit on Schedule A, so there is no labour certification — the employer files the I-140 petition directly. You cannot apply for it yourself. You need a BSc in Nursing, a pass in the NCLEX exam, the VisaScreen certificate, and an English test.

The wait: the November 2026 Visa Bulletin shows green cards reaching petitions filed before 15 June 2024 — so a new applicant waits around two to three years. The clock starts when the I-140 is filed, and preparation happens before that.

USA — Route 2: H-1B Visa (no lottery)

A temporary work visa through non-profit hospitals, so there is no lottery and it can be filed at any time of year. You can start working in the USA within months, while your green card keeps processing in the background. It needs the NCLEX already passed, plus an English test and VisaScreen. One agency, MedPro, starts nurses at over $35 an hour, with a $7,000 sign-on bonus and raises in the first 30 months.

The NCLEX exam

The NCLEX-RN is the licensing exam for both the USA and Canada. There is no percentage and no fixed passing mark — it is a computer adaptive test. The passing standard is 0.00 logits, with 85 to 150 questions in five hours, including 15 unscored trial questions. In Pakistan the test centre is the Pearson Professional Center, Mall of Lahore. There are no refunds, and a retake costs the full fee again.

Canada — the route

Canada works differently: licence first, then a job offer, then a work permit or permanent residence. First the NNAS assessment — $750 CAD expedited, with the report in five business days once documents arrive. Documents must come directly from your school, the nursing council and your employers, never from you. Then NCLEX and English, then registration in a province.

British Columbia is the easiest province: Health Match BC is free, with free nurse navigators, and an IEN bursary of up to $17,000. A job offer from a BC health authority fast-tracks permanent residence. Ontario needs the NCLEX plus a jurisprudence exam and a transition course — and its supervised practice programme only covers gaps up to five years, so a longer gap needs re-entry confirmed with the regulator. Alberta’s health pathway adds 600 points to your Express Entry score with a job offer.

Warning: Canada’s Express Entry healthcare category now needs 12 months of experience in the last three years — so it is closed until you work again for a year. Recent healthcare draws invited scores around 467 to 475.

Official starting points: NNAS · Health Match BC · NCSBN / NCLEX · CGFNS / VisaScreen

What starts now

Three things: book the IELTS, start NCLEX preparation, and return to a hospital job for six to twelve months — agencies want recent inpatient experience, and Canada’s Express Entry needs 12 months in the last three years. Keep your documents ready: degrees and transcripts, your nursing council licence and verification, experience letters from every hospital, your passport, and your English and NCLEX results as they come.
